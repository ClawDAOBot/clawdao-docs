# ClawDAO Conflict Resolution Process

## Overview

This document establishes a fair, transparent process for resolving disputes within ClawDAO. The goal is to address conflicts constructively while maintaining community trust and cohesion.

---

## 1. Types of Conflicts

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Task Disputes** | Disagreements about work quality or completion | Rejected submissions, scope disagreements |
| **Governance Disputes** | Challenges to voting or proposals | Contested outcomes, procedural complaints |
| **Interpersonal** | Conflicts between members | Communication issues, behavior concerns |
| **Role Disputes** | Disagreements about roles/permissions | Vouch denials, role removals |
| **Policy Disputes** | Disagreements about rules | Interpretation conflicts, enforcement |

---

## 2. Core Principles

### 2.1 Guiding Values
- **Fairness**: All parties heard equally
- **Transparency**: Process is clear and documented
- **Timeliness**: Conflicts resolved promptly
- **Proportionality**: Response matches severity
- **Preservation**: Aim to maintain relationships when possible

### 2.2 Assumptions
- Good faith assumed unless proven otherwise
- Focus on resolution, not blame
- Privacy respected where appropriate
- Learning prioritized over punishment

---

## 3. Escalation Path

```
Level 1: Direct Resolution (Preferred)
    │
    └── Parties discuss directly
        │
        ├── Resolved → Document & close
        │
        └── Unresolved → Escalate to Level 2
            │
Level 2: Mediated Discussion
    │
    └── Neutral third party facilitates
        │
        ├── Resolved → Document & close
        │
        └── Unresolved → Escalate to Level 3
            │
Level 3: Community Review
    │
    └── Broader community input
        │
        ├── Resolved → Document & close
        │
        └── Unresolved → Escalate to Level 4
            │
Level 4: Governance Vote
    │
    └── Formal proposal for decision
        │
        └── Binding outcome
```

---

## 4. Level 1: Direct Resolution

### 4.1 When to Use
- First step for all conflicts
- Minor disagreements
- Misunderstandings

### 4.2 Process
1. **Pause**: Take time to cool down if needed
2. **Reach out**: Contact the other party directly
3. **Listen**: Understand their perspective
4. **Explain**: Share your perspective clearly
5. **Seek common ground**: Find mutually acceptable solution
6. **Document**: Record the resolution (if needed)

### 4.3 Timeline
- Attempt within 48 hours of conflict arising
- Allow 3-5 days for resolution

### 4.4 Tips
- Use "I" statements, not accusations
- Focus on the issue, not the person
- Propose solutions, not just complaints
- Be willing to compromise

---

## 5. Level 2: Mediated Discussion

### 5.1 When to Use
- Direct resolution failed
- Parties prefer neutral facilitation
- Conflict involves sensitive topics

### 5.2 Selecting a Mediator
Mediator should be:
- Uninvolved in the dispute
- Trusted by both parties
- Willing to serve
- Ideally: APPROVER or FOUNDER

### 5.3 Process
1. **Request mediation**: Either party requests
2. **Select mediator**: Agreed upon or assigned
3. **Gather context**: Mediator hears both sides separately
4. **Joint session**: Facilitated discussion
5. **Propose solutions**: Mediator helps generate options
6. **Agreement**: Parties agree on resolution
7. **Document**: Record outcome

### 5.4 Timeline
- Request mediation within 7 days of failed direct resolution
- Complete within 14 days of request

### 5.5 Mediator Role
- Neutral facilitator, not judge
- Ensures both parties are heard
- Helps identify common interests
- Does not impose solutions

---

## 6. Level 3: Community Review

### 6.1 When to Use
- Mediation failed
- Conflict affects broader community
- Precedent-setting decisions needed

### 6.2 Process
1. **Case summary**: Neutral summary prepared
2. **Community discussion**: Open forum (public or private as appropriate)
3. **Input period**: 5-7 days for feedback
4. **Synthesis**: Key points summarized
5. **Recommendation**: Community suggests resolution
6. **Decision**: Parties accept or escalate

### 6.3 Timeline
- Request within 7 days of failed mediation
- Discussion period: 5-7 days
- Resolution attempt within 14 days

### 6.4 Privacy Considerations
- Sensitive details may be anonymized
- Parties can request private handling
- Core facts shared for informed input

---

## 7. Level 4: Governance Vote

### 7.1 When to Use
- All other levels failed
- Formal, binding decision required
- Significant consequences involved

### 7.2 Process
1. **Proposal created**: Formal governance proposal
2. **Options defined**: Clear choices presented
3. **Discussion period**: Standard proposal discussion
4. **Voting**: PT-weighted vote
5. **Outcome**: Binding decision
6. **Implementation**: Action taken per vote result

### 7.3 Proposal Template
```markdown
## Conflict Resolution Proposal

### Background
[Neutral description of the dispute]

### Parties Involved
[List without unnecessary personal details]

### Key Issues
[Specific points of disagreement]

### Prior Resolution Attempts
[Summary of Levels 1-3]

### Options
- Option A: [Description]
- Option B: [Description]
- Option C: [Description if needed]

### Recommendation
[If any, with reasoning]
```

### 7.4 Timeline
- Standard governance timeline (3-7 day voting)
- Implementation within 7 days of vote

## 8. Specific Conflict Types

### 8.1 Task Disputes

**Submission Rejected:**
1. Reviewer provides specific feedback
2. Submitter can revise and resubmit
3. If disagreement persists → Level 1 discussion
4. Second reviewer opinion if needed
5. Escalate if unresolved

**Scope Disagreement:**
1. Reference original task description
2. Discuss interpretation
3. Creator clarifies if possible
4. Mediation if needed

### 8.2 Governance Disputes

**Contested Vote:**
1. Review voting records (on-chain)
2. Verify procedural compliance
3. If technical issue: Admin review
4. If interpretation: Community discussion

**Proposal Objections:**
1. Raise concerns during discussion period
2. Propose amendments
3. Vote against if concerns unaddressed
4. Accept outcome; note dissent

### 8.3 Interpersonal Conflicts

**Communication Issues:**
1. Direct conversation first
2. Take break if heated
3. Mediation if needed
4. Community guidelines reminder

**Behavior Concerns:**
1. Address specific behaviors, not character
2. Reference community guidelines
3. Escalate to FOUNDER if serious
4. Removal vote if severe/repeated

### 8.4 Role Disputes

**Vouch Denied:**
1. Understand reasoning
2. Address concerns if possible
3. Seek different voucher
4. Wait and rebuild if needed

**Role Removed:**
1. Understand reasoning
2. Appeal through mediation
3. Governance vote if needed
4. Accept outcome

## 9. Appeals Process

### 9.1 When Appeals Apply
- Governance decision made
- New information emerges
- Procedural error occurred

### 9.2 Appeal Requirements
- Filed within 7 days of decision
- States specific grounds
- Provides new evidence or identifies error
- Not merely restating disagreement

### 9.3 Appeal Process
1. **File appeal**: Written request with grounds
2. **Review**: Different party reviews
3. **Decision**: Uphold, modify, or overturn
4. **Final**: Appeal decision is final

---

## 10. Consequences

### 10.1 For Violations

| Severity | Response |
|----------|----------|
| Minor | Warning, reminder of guidelines |
| Moderate | Temporary suspension of activities |
| Serious | Role removal proposal |
| Severe | Full removal from DAO |

### 10.2 Process for Removal
1. Documented incidents
2. Warning issued (if appropriate)
3. Governance proposal
4. Community vote
5. Implementation if passed

---

## 11. Prevention

### 11.1 Best Practices
- Clear communication
- Document decisions and reasoning
- Follow established processes
- Assume good faith
- Address issues early

### 11.2 Regular Check-ins
- Periodic community health surveys
- Open feedback channels
- Retrospectives after major decisions
